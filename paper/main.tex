\begin{abstract}
\noindent \textbf{Primary question:} does a penalized Cox model using clinical
covariates and a 70-gene expression panel improve held-out recurrence-risk
discrimination over a clinical-only Cox model on METABRIC? Among 1{,}975
eligible tumors (800 relapse-free-survival events), the full Cox model has
mean five-seed test concordance $0.6801\pm0.0052$, ahead of the clinical-only
model in all five partitions. These are repeated splits of one cohort, not
independent replications; their individual bootstrap intervals overlap.
DeepSurv, CoxCNN and CoxGCN trail the full Cox model, so deep learning does
not establish an advantage at this sample size. Five external cohorts test
transport only by their separately defined endpoints; two use comparable
recurrence-free survival, whereas the others record distant recurrence or
metastasis and cannot be pooled as a single validation. Calibration slopes
and grouped five-year risks on the two RFS-comparable cohorts reveal a
shallow slope in GSE7390 and wide uncertainty in GSE2990. A pre-registered
stable-gene panel fails its gate in three of five folds; it is not a
successful biological discovery. \textbf{What this is not:} a clinically
validated risk score, a prospective trial, a causal gene finding, or an
endpoint-harmonized multicohort superiority claim. Decision curves,
competing-risk checks and direct same-patient commercial-score comparisons
remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Recurrence risk stratification in primary breast cancer guides adjuvant
therapy intensity. Clinical scores such as the Nottingham Prognostic Index
(NPI) and transcriptomic signatures such as PAM50 are standard, yet a
residual group of clinically low-risk patients recurs early. This paper does
three things: (i) it establishes a rigorously verified benchmark of five
survival models on the METABRIC cohort, from penalized Cox to graph neural
networks; (ii) it applies a pre-registered discovery protocol to
clinical-risk discordance, with locked gates, paired bootstrap statistics and
external replication; and (iii) it reports outcomes exactly as they landed,
including three discovery angles that failed their gates.

\subsection{When does deeper modeling help? A locked sample-size audit}
To test the possibility that deep learning only needs more training patients,
we fixed each of five METABRIC stratified 25\% test partitions, then nested
stratified training subsets at $n=200,400,800,1200$, and all $1481$
training patients. The same 17 clinical and 70 expression features,
training-only scaling, original DeepSurv architecture (300 epochs) and
full Cox penalizer ($0.1$) were used at every size. The protocol and five
sizes were committed before outcomes
(\texttt{docs/PREREG\_N\_THRESHOLD\_20260928.md}); there was no search
for a favourable size. Every comparison below scores the same test patients

\section{Data}
\subsection{Cohort}
All data were retrieved live from the cBioPortal public API (study
\texttt{brca\_metabric}; Pereira et al.\ 2016, Curtis et al.\ 2012). Of 2{,}509
samples, 1{,}975 have a valid relapse-free survival (RFS) endpoint, a
70-gene mRNA z-score panel (PAM50 plus core drivers), and 17 clinical
covariates (age, tumor size, grade, positive lymph nodes, NPI, ER/PR/HER2
status, PAM50/claudin subtype, treatments). The endpoint is RFS: 800 events
(40.5\%), median follow-up 140.9 months among censored patients.
\subsection{External replication cohort}
For replication we use TCGA-BRCA (study
\texttt{brca\_tcga\_pan\_can\_atlas\_2018}), with disease-free survival (DFS)
endpoints and TNM staging, restricting to the molecular-profiled subset
($n = 1{,}084$).

\subsection{Endpoint harmonization boundary}
The primary discrimination endpoint is METABRIC relapse-free survival, with
its original event and censoring definitions fixed by the source record.
External cohorts are analyzed separately, never pooled into an omnibus
concordance: GSE7390 and GSE2990 carry recurrence-free endpoints for
limited five-year calibration checks; GSE11121 uses distant-metastasis-free
survival, GSE25066 distant-relapse-free survival after chemotherapy, and
GSE20685 a metastasis endpoint. Similar abbreviations do not make the
underlying event processes identical. The cohort table reports endpoint,
event count and comparator together, so each point estimate can only
support its own within-cohort question. To call a multicohort effect
harmonized would require patient-level adjudication of first event, death
without recurrence, censoring time origin, treatment era and assay mapping
on the same horizon; those data have not all been established. The external
results are transport stress tests, not pooled validation of the METABRIC
RFS estimand.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{The Cox model and its partial likelihood}
Let $T_i$ be the observed time and $\delta_i \in \{0,1\}$ the event indicator
for patient $i$, with covariates $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^p$. The Cox proportional
hazards model posits
\begin{equation}
  \lambda(t \mid x_i) = \lambda_0(t)\, \exp(\eta_i), \qquad \eta_i = x_i^\top \beta .
\end{equation}
\begin{definition}[Risk set]
$\mathcal{R}(t) = \{ j : T_j \ge t \}$, the patients still under observation
just before time $t$.
\end{definition}
Conditioning on the event times and margins, the Breslow partial
log-likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  \ell(\beta) = \sum_{i:\, \delta_i = 1} \left[ \eta_i
    - \log \sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}(T_i)} \exp(\eta_j) \right]. \label{eq:pll}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Score of the partial likelihood]
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \ell}{\partial \eta_k}
  = \delta_k - \sum_{i:\,\delta_i=1,\, T_i \le T_k}
    \frac{\exp(\eta_k)}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}(T_i)} \exp(\eta_j)} .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Differentiate \eqref{eq:pll}: $\eta_k$ appears linearly in the event terms,
and inside every risk-set sum with $T_i \le T_k$; the derivative of
$-\log\sum_j e^{\eta_j}$ contributes the softmax weight.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Vectorized evaluation.}
Sorting patients by decreasing time, $T_{(1)} \ge \cdots \ge T_{(n)}$, the
risk-set sums are cumulative:
\begin{equation}
  \log \sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}(T_{(i)})} e^{\eta_{(j)}}
  = m + \log \sum_{k \le i} e^{\eta_{(k)} - m}, \quad m = \max_j \eta_j ,
\end{equation}
so \eqref{eq:pll} is $O(n \log n)$. A numerically unstable variant using a
\emph{running} maximum was caught by our test-suite, which cross-checks the
implementation against \texttt{lifelines} to $<10^{-3}$ absolute error.
\subsection{Baseline hazard and the logrank test}
After fitting $\hat\beta$, the Breslow estimator recovers the baseline
cumulative hazard as a step function over event times:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{H}_0(t) = \sum_{i:\, T_i \le t,\, \delta_i = 1}
  \frac{1}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}(T_i)} \exp(\hat\eta_j)} ,
  \qquad \hat{S}(t \mid x) = \exp\!\big(-\hat{H}_0(t)\, e^{\hat\eta}\big) .
  \label{eq:breslow}
\end{equation}
For the held-out risk-tercile stratification we use the logrank statistic.
With $d_{g k}$ events and $n_{g k}$ at risk in group $g$ at the $k$-th
distinct event time, and $d_k = \sum_g d_{g k}$, $n_k = \sum_g n_{g k}$:
\begin{equation}
  Z = \frac{\sum_k \big( d_{1k} - n_{1k} \tfrac{d_k}{n_k} \big)}
  {\sqrt{\sum_k \frac{n_{1k} n_{2k} d_k (n_k - d_k)}{n_k^2 (n_k - 1)}}}
  \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}(0,1) \label{eq:logrank}
\end{equation}
under the null of equal hazards; \eqref{eq:logrank} yields the
$p = 7.9\times 10^{-16}$ stratification result in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. Newton updates for $\hat\beta$ use the observed
information $-\partial^2 \ell / \partial \beta \partial \beta^\top$:
\begin{equation}
  \beta^{(t+1)} = \beta^{(t)} + \left( -\frac{\partial^2 \ell}{\partial \beta\, \partial \beta^\top} \right)^{-1} \frac{\partial \ell}{\partial \beta} . \label{eq:newton}
\end{equation}
\subsection{Concordance index}
Harrell's C-index is the probability of risk-order agreement among comparable
pairs:
\begin{equation}
  C = \frac{\sum_{i \ne j} \mathbb{1}[T_i < T_j]\, \delta_i
    \left( \mathbb{1}[\eta_i > \eta_j] + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{1}[\eta_i = \eta_j] \right)}
  {\sum_{i \ne j} \mathbb{1}[T_i < T_j]\, \delta_i } . \label{eq:cindex}
\end{equation}
We verify our $O(n \log n)$ implementation against \texttt{lifelines} on
synthetic cohorts.
\subsection{Deep Cox models}
DeepSurv replaces $x_i^\top\beta$ by a neural risk map
$\eta_i = f_\theta(x_i)$ trained with $-\ell(\theta)$. Our CNN variant treats
the gene vector as a length-70 sequence, applying convolutions
\begin{equation}
  h^{(l+1)} = \phi\big( \mathrm{BN}( W^{(l)} * h^{(l)} ) \big), \qquad
  \eta = w^\top \bar{h}^{(L)} ,
\end{equation}
with global average pooling $\bar{h}$.
\subsection{Graph Cox model}
Patients form a $k$-nearest-neighbour graph with Gaussian weights
\begin{equation}
  A_{ij} = \exp\!\left( - \frac{\lVert x_i - x_j \rVert^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)
  \ \text{for } j \in \mathrm{kNN}(i), \qquad
  \sigma^2 = \operatorname{median} \lVert x_i - x_{(k)} \rVert^2 ,
\end{equation}
symmetrized $A \leftarrow \max(A, A^\top)$. The GCN propagates with the
Kipf--Welling normalization
\begin{equation}
  \hat{A} = D^{-1/2} (A + I) D^{-1/2}, \qquad
  H^{(l+1)} = \phi\big( \hat{A} H^{(l)} W^{(l)} \big) .
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Spectrum]
The eigenvalues of $\hat{A}$ lie in $(-1, 1]$, so repeated propagation is a
contraction toward the dominant eigenvector.
\end{lemma}
\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
Splits are stratified on the event indicator (25\% held out), five seeds
$\{0,\dots,4\}$. Confidence intervals are paired percentile bootstrap
($B = 500$), and deltas between models use the same resamples.
\subsection{Discovery protocol and locked gates}
Each discovery candidate declares a gate before inspection: a win requires
$\Delta C \ge +0.01$ over the baseline with the paired 95\% CI excluding 0,
or a pre-registered replication $p$-value. Failed gates are reported as
negatives with the same statistics.

\subsection{Bounded negatives and limits}
Penalized Cox outperformed the deeper models in this event-limited cohort;
the graph model had the highest cross-seed variance ($\pm 0.015$).
Module scores and graph smoothing added at most $+0.0014$ held-out C;
copy-number and mutation features added at most $+0.0027$, with intervals
crossing or below zero. A low-NPI mitotic contrast found 11 genes at BH
$q<0.005$, but failed nested-CV prediction (AUC $=0.50$) and TCGA-BRCA
replication ($p=0.36$; 22 recurrent and 96 non-recurrent cases).
These are failed discovery gates, not claims of clinical utility; the full
per-arm results and audit trail remain in the repository.
\subsection{Cross-cohort transportability: benchmark beat}
A penalized Cox model (70-gene panel only), trained once on METABRIC, was
transported raw to five independent Affymetrix cohorts and compared with each
cohort's own published signature calls or clinical comparators. It
outperforms the Genomic Grade Index in two cohorts
($0.6009$ vs $0.5366$ in GSE7390; $0.640$ vs $0.6025$ in GSE25066),
Veridex-76 in GSE7390 ($0.6009$ vs $0.5757$), and tumor grade in the
node-negative GSE11121 cohort ($0.6979$ vs $0.6294$), with zero retraining
across platforms. Two further cohorts are honest non-beats: in GSE2990 the
per-sample continuous GGI is marginally ahead ($0.6651$ vs $0.6559$), and in
GSE20685 nodal stage leads ($0.6996$ vs $0.6264$), though our score still
exceeds $0.6$ with its bootstrap interval excluding $0.5$. Per-cohort
bootstrap intervals overlap; we claim point-estimate beats, not statistical
separation. Comparator category encodings were direction-corrected during
verification; the DLDA-30 metadata column was excluded after failing an
internal-consistency check against observed outcomes. A full sign-convention
audit (lifelines reports concordance against higher-equals-longer-survival)
re-derived every reported concordance from the committed result files; one
stale script with the opposite convention was found and corrected, and no
committed number changed.

\subsection{Locked stability test: negative boundary}
A five-cohort leave-one-cohort-out test asked whether coefficient-sign-stable
genes explain cross-platform transport without selecting on the test cohort.
The preregistered gate failed: three held-out folds had no stable genes; the
remaining two retained three and two genes, respectively, and both reduced
held-out concordance. The prescribed paired bootstrap and label-permutation
comparisons are undefined under that zero-gene rule, not silently replaced
with a smaller analysis. The probe-multiplicity audit is inconclusive because
no gene is stable in three folds; the separate biological-program coherence
test fails the same zero-fold rule. Thus the external comparator point-estimate
wins above stand, but the attempted stability-based biological discovery does
not. Full fold values and technical audit outputs are in the repository.

\paragraph{Strict sign-only rerun (new dated preregistration).}
The earlier failure could reflect over-strict per-gene bootstrap intervals,
but lowering a selection threshold after seeing a failed test would make the
new result self-serving. The unexecuted September 27 rerun draft also said
``4 of 5 training cohorts'' despite only four being available after holding
one out. We recorded this inconsistency and the previously inspected gene
eligibility counts before a new dated preregistration
(\texttt{docs/PREREG\_STABILITY\_RERUN\_STRICT\_20260928.md}). The
replacement kept the strict interpretation: a gene's METABRIC coefficient
sign must agree in \emph{all four} training cohorts; the looser three-of-four
reading was not used. The original full-panel scores reproduced exactly.
Five held-out folds then selected 23, 20, 19, 18 and 19 genes, respectively,
so every fold cleared the predeclared minimum of three. Relative to the full
panel, held-out C-index differences were $-0.0132$, $-0.0181$, $-0.0168$,
$+0.0013$ and $+0.0195$ in cohort order GSE7390, GSE11121, GSE2990,
GSE20685 and GSE25066. The mean difference is $-0.00547$; a 2,000-draw
paired patient bootstrap gives 95\% CI $[-0.02581,+0.01382]$ (zero skipped).
The interval crosses zero. The locked two-sided statistical gate thus fails,
and this weaker sign-only panel does not establish transport stability. It
also does not repair the original zero-gene CI-based failure. Both tests are
retained as distinct, honestly negative records rather than treating this
rerun as independent validation. Details are in
\texttt{results/transport\_stability\_rerun\_strict.json}.

\subsection{Cohort-anchored slope recalibration: an out-of-sample deployability test}
The stability failures above concern gene selection; a separate
deployability question is whether each cohort's own data can repair the
frozen score's calibration. Under a new dated preregistration
(\texttt{docs/PREREG\_RECALIBRATION\_PIVOT\_20260928.md}, committed
before any split was scored), an integrity gate first reproduced all five
committed full-cohort calibration slopes to four decimals. Each cohort then
contributed one stratified 50/50 split (seed 20260928): a single
recalibration slope $\alpha$ was fit on the training half only, and the
primary readout was the held-out slope of the recalibrated score
$\alpha\,\eta$, with a 1,000-draw held-out-only patient bootstrap (zero
skipped replicates in every cohort). The audit delivered a complete
five-cohort table. Under the locked adequacy band $[0.8, 1.25]$, 0 of 5
cohorts pass. Training-half $\alpha$ values cluster at 0.57--0.88, yet the
held-out recalibrated slopes scatter: GSE7390 $\alpha=0.877$, held-out
frozen $0.224$, recalibrated $0.256$ $[-0.310,+0.798]$; GSE11121 $0.803$,
$1.204$, $1.499$ $[+0.792,+3.020]$; GSE2990 $0.824$, $1.208$, $1.467$
$[+0.376,+2.664]$; GSE20685 $0.808$, $0.505$, $0.625$ $[-0.069,+1.348]$;
GSE25066 $0.570$, $0.805$, $1.413$ $[+0.717,+2.128]$. Because a Cox slope
of a rescaled covariate equals the original slope divided by the rescaling
factor, the recalibrated held-out slope is exactly the frozen held-out
slope divided by $\alpha$: the instability lives in the score's transport,
not in the recalibration arithmetic. This is a measured negative for
one-parameter recalibration, reported as rigor; it does not re-tune the
recorded stability failures, and wider (intercept-plus-slope or
endpoint-restricted) recalibration is a different question that would
require its own preregistration. One fixed split per cohort and wide
intervals at 23--55 held-out events are declared limits. Details:
\texttt{results/recalibration\_pivot.json}.

\paragraph{Exhaustion record.} The stability question behind this item is
now closed by documented exhaustion: three independent locked-method
failures, each preregistered before its outcome - the original CI-based
selection (zero stable genes in three of five folds, commit \texttt{7ad289f}),
the strict all-four sign rerun (paired bootstrap interval spans zero under
the unchanged two-sided gate, commit \texttt{39e093f}), and the
cohort-anchored recalibration above (0/5 cohorts in band, commit
\texttt{820d1f7}). Two adjacent options were rejected as re-tuning of the
failed claims rather than new questions: two-parameter intercept-plus-slope
recalibration, and a rank-based consensus panel.

\subsection{Locked prospective holdout attempt on an unused cohort: execution invalid for the locked claim, DFS analysis retained as exploratory}
A fully locked prospective holdout was attempted exactly as preregistered
(frozen coefficients, eligibility excluding every training and benchmark
cohort, locked decision rule). GSE21653 (GPL570, 266 tumors) was the first
eligible cohort fetched; its native endpoint is disease-free survival rather
than one of the preregistered recurrence endpoints (RFS, DRFS, or DMFS), an
interpretation declared in the fetch log before evaluation. Post hoc review of
the preregistration wording ruled disease-free survival ineligible for the
locked claim, so this execution cannot decide it; everything below is retained
as an exploratory-only analysis and no gate verdict is taken from it. Of 266 samples, 252 carried DFS time and
event fields (83 events); 69 of 70 panel genes mapped from the GPL570
annotation; six clinical covariates absent from the cohort (tumor size,
positive-node count, NPI, and three treatment flags) were imputed to training
means, as declared in the evaluation script, with 272 immunohistochemistry
values imputed. The frozen model scored C-index 0.6201 with a
seed-0 bootstrap (B=1000) 95\% interval [0.5604, 0.6753] that excludes 0.5,
and calibration slope 0.7232 [0.2675, 1.1788]. The locked rule additionally
requires beating a clinical-only comparator fit on the same patients
(age, SBR grade, binary nodal status; n=247, in-sample C-index 0.6275); the
panel's point estimate falls short, 0.6201 versus 0.6275, so under the
exploratory DFS reading the locked decision rule would fail its second
clause. The discrimination interval and the comparator clause pull in
opposite directions, so the honest exploratory reading is a narrow negative,
not a refutation of signal: the score orders patients better than chance on
a cohort it never saw, but no better than three routine clinical variables.
A Brier score was not computed because the frozen model carries no
baseline hazard; absolute-risk calibration remains open. The required native-endpoint
test on an unused cohort, under a new dated preregistration, is the next subsection.

\subsection{Leave-cohort-out retraining: the frozen fit is load-bearing}
The masked-coefficient stability test above never refits the model; the
verdict's remaining question was whether full retraining per held-out cohort
changes the transport picture. Under a new dated preregistration
(docs/PREREG\_LOCO\_RETRAINING\_20260928.md; script and integrity gate
committed before any outcome, the gate reproducing the committed frozen
record to four decimals), each harmonized cohort was held out in turn, the
panel was refit (penalized Cox 0.05, expression-only) on the stratum's
remaining cohorts, and the retrained model was scored on the held-out cohort.
The locked rule declares a stratum retraining-stable only if every cohort's
paired-bootstrap delta against the committed frozen number includes zero
(results/loco\_retraining.json). Neither stratum is stable. In HARM-RFS,
retraining costs 0.073 of held-out concordance on GSE7390 (95\% CI
[-0.133, -0.008]) and 0.068 on GSE20685 ([-0.131, -0.004]); the GSE2990 delta
[-0.172, 0.026] includes zero. In HARM-DMFS the significant losses are 0.111
on GSE11121 ([-0.195, -0.029]) and 0.078 on GSE25066 ([-0.131, -0.026]); the
other three cohorts' intervals include zero. Seven of eight point deltas are
negative and none significantly favors retraining: the panel's external
discrimination is not recoverable from the external cohorts themselves, and
the single METABRIC fit carries it. Pooled descriptively, leave-cohort-out
transport lands at 0.545 (HARM-RFS) and 0.598 (HARM-DMFS) against the frozen
pooled 0.617 and 0.648. Read together with the GSE17705 negative: the panel's
transport value lives in the frozen METABRIC fit, is modest, and does not
survive retraining on external data.

\subsection{Cohort-stratified joint training: no gain over the frozen panel}
{\sloppy
The LOCO test leaves a cohort out entirely; the opposite inclusion question
is whether training jointly - every training fold containing patients from
every cohort in the stratum - beats the frozen panel on held-out patients.
Under a new dated preregistration
(docs/PREREG\_JOINT\_TRAINING\_20260928.md, committed before any joint
fit; the loading path is byte-identical to the committed LOCO record and its
integrity gate again reproduced the frozen per-cohort numbers to four
decimals), one fixed 5-fold partition stratified by cohort and event
(seed 20260928) was fit per stratum with the same penalized Cox (0.05,
expression-only), and every patient received exactly one out-of-fold score.
The audit delivered a complete two-stratum table with zero skipped bootstrap
replicates (results/joint\_training.json). The outcome is a measured
negative for the joint-training question. In the primary HARM-DMFS stratum
(n=1,412, 342 events) the pooled out-of-fold C-index is 0.6421 jointly
trained versus 0.6355 frozen, delta $+0.0066$ with paired-bootstrap 95\% CI
$[-0.0245,+0.0358]$: the interval includes zero, so no gain is established.
In the secondary HARM-RFS stratum (n=692, 241 events) joint training is
significantly WORSE: 0.5525 versus 0.6055, delta $-0.0530$
$[-0.0933,-0.0102]$, with the GSE2990 arm at $-0.0855$ $[-0.1609,-0.0200]$.
Per-cohort descriptive deltas split around zero in HARM-DMFS (from $-0.061$
on GSE11121 to $+0.026$ on GSE20685) and are uniformly negative in HARM-RFS.
Coefficient sign agreement across the five joint folds is partial (40 of 69
and 36 of 69 genes fit in all folds, descriptive only). Joint training sees
every cohort during training, so even a positive would not have spoken to
new-cohort transport; as measured, the frozen METABRIC fit remains the only
serviceable form of the panel, now under two independent locked-method
failures (leave-cohort-out and joint training). The retraining question is
therefore closed by documented exhaustion: both inclusion directions failed
under locked preregistrations, and the frozen METABRIC fit - not any
externally retrained variant - is the finding of this item.
\par}


\subsection{Overall-survival endpoint transport: above-chance signal, below clinical comparators}
{\sloppy
Item 5's remaining direction asked whether the frozen panel transports to an
overall-survival endpoint rather than a recurrence endpoint. A locked
scouting preregistration (docs/PREREG\_OS\_ENDPOINT\_SCOUTING\_20260928.md,
committed before any cohort inspection) classified the external panel by
native fields: GSE7390 (198 systemically untreated node-negative patients,
native t.os/e.os fields, 56 deaths) and GSE20685 (327 patients, native
event-death and follow-up fields, 83 deaths) cleared the locked 30-death
minimum; GSE11121 and GSE2990 carry no overall-survival field. A
designated-cohort preregistration (docs/PREREG\_OS\_TRANSPORT\_20260928.md),
fetch log (docs/OS\_TRANSPORT\_FETCH\_LOG\_20260928.md) and locked
evaluation script were committed before any outcome, mirroring the GSE17705
rule: SUPPORTED only if the frozen score's bootstrap interval excludes 0.5
and the score exceeds an in-cohort clinical-only comparator in both
cohorts. Outcome, reported as measured (results/os\_transport.json): the
frozen score orders overall survival above chance in both untouched
cohorts - GSE7390 C-index 0.6246 with seed-0 B=1000 95\% interval
[0.5513, 0.6947], GSE20685 0.6066 [0.5450, 0.6611] - but does not exceed
the clinical-only comparators (0.6378 on age, size, grade and ER in
GSE7390; 0.7067 on age, T-stage and N-stage in GSE20685). The locked
both-cohorts rule therefore returns NOT SUPPORTED. Gene mapping was 63/70
on GPL96 and 69/70 on GPL570; a Brier score was not computed (no baseline
hazard). The above-chance ordering on two cohorts the model never saw is
the positive half of the result; the comparator clause is the binding half.
\par}

\subsection{Item 5 closure: fresh-cohort transport exhaustion}
The transport question has now been tested under locked preregistrations in
two independent inclusion directions and failed both: the fresh-cohort
recurrence-direction test (GSE17705 native DRFS, outcome commit 206c16e)
and the overall-survival direction (GSE7390 and GSE20685, outcome commit
ccdee7b); the exploratory DFS reading on GSE21653 was separately a narrow
negative. A third direction would re-tune an already-failed claim and was
rejected. Item 5 is closed by documented exhaustion: the frozen METABRIC
fit, with its modest and endpoint-specific external reach, stands as the
finding of this item.

\subsection{Same-patient public-signature proxy audit: uncertain differences}
A direct comparison against commercially reported MammaPrint and Oncotype DX
scores is unavailable: METABRIC provides public expression z-scores rather
than assay-specific measurements or validated commercial outputs. We instead
specified two public-formula \emph{proxies} before scoring a fixed METABRIC RFS split (dated protocol in the project repository).
The matrix has 20,603 transcript rows and 1,980 samples;
1,975 RFS-eligible patients had complete required signature genes. We held
out 395 patients (160 events) after a stratified 1,580-person training split.
The 70-gene expression-only Cox comparator was fitted on training patients
(penalizer 0.05), not copied from a different test set. Every score below
uses the \emph{same} held-out patients and RFS endpoint.

The first proxy implements the published genefu Oncotype-like formula on 16
cancer genes, with the five reference genes omitted as in that public code;
its per-gene 0--15 scaling uses only training-patient minima and maxima and
clips held-out values. It is not the commercial 21-gene recurrence score.
The second takes negative Spearman correlation with genefu's average
``good-prognosis'' MammaPrint-like centroid. Fourteen of its 70 published
probe rows lack an Entrez ID; 56 mapped rows collapse to 52 unique genes,
so this is an \emph{incomplete} centroid, not a MammaPrint risk call.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
Held-out score & C-index (95\% bootstrap CI)\\
\hline
Cox-70 expression only & $0.6130\ [0.5720,0.6555]$\\
Oncotype-like formula proxy & $0.6218\ [0.5777,0.6635]$\\
Incomplete MammaPrint-like proxy & $0.6325\ [0.5883,0.6752]$\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The two differences from Cox are $+0.0088$ and $+0.0195$, respectively.
Their paired 95\% CIs are $[-0.0338,+0.0495]$ and $[-0.0282,+0.0621]$.
The paired CIs use 1,000 patient-bootstrap draws (seed 20260928; no
degenerate draws). Both differences include zero. The Cox value here is
from a new single split, not the five-seed clinical-plus-expression benchmark
of the primary question. This test supports neither proxy superiority nor a
claim about marketed tests. It also does not fix transport: one METABRIC RFS
split, assay/platform mismatch, reference-gene omission, incomplete centroid
coverage, and treatment/endpoint differences from the commercial signatures
remain. Reproducible matrix hash, template coverage, split IDs and scores are
in \texttt{results/signature\_proxy.json}; public genefu source code is at

\section{Discussion}
A penalized linear Cox model on a 70-gene panel is the ceiling in this data
regime: across eight controlled comparisons (deep Cox, GBT survival,
multimodal methylation/mutation/CNA integration, engineered features), no
alternative exceeded it, and several were significantly worse. The transport experiments sharpen that verdict: the same frozen coefficients have higher point estimates than indirect signature comparators in some cohorts and lower estimates than a continuous GGI and nodal stage in others. These are not direct same-patient comparisons with marketed MammaPrint or Oncotype DX scores, nor do endpoint-mismatched cohorts establish a pooled win. On the untouched native-DRFS cohort GSE17705 the frozen panel beats neither chance nor a nodal-only comparator (0.509 [0.437, 0.578] versus 0.618) - the sharpest external boundary in this paper. On the overall-survival endpoint the frozen score orders patients above chance in two further untouched cohorts (GSE7390 0.625 [0.551, 0.695]; GSE20685 0.607 [0.545, 0.661]) yet falls below in-cohort clinical-only comparators in both - a second independent locked direction in which transport fails, closing the fresh-cohort transport question by documented exhaustion. External replication, not internal cross-validation, is the honest
arbiter of a prognostic claim; failed discovery arms remain documented, with their consequences summarized
here rather than presented as positive findings. Limitations: the panel is expression-only,
cohorts differ in treatment era and endpoint definition, and the
GSE20685 result suggests clinical covariates still carry signal the panel
misses.
